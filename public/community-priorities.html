Civic & Policy/Community priorities brought to congressional offices
Community Priorities Survey · May 2026

What 113 Mongolians across the U.S. told us.

In May 2026, Mongolian Americans came together through a series of town halls and a rapid pulse survey to name the challenges our community faces and what we want Congress to hear. The result is the Mongolian American Community Policy Priorities.

How these priorities came together

From community town halls to the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus.

01 · ConveneTown hallsMongolian community members met in a series of town halls to talk through the challenges they face.
02 · ListenRapid pulse surveyA short survey in Mongolian and English reached 113 people across 17 states and D.C.
03 · AnalyzeVolunteer-led analysisCommunity volunteers processed the data together and drafted the Mongolian American Community Policy Priorities.
04 · EngageOn Capitol HillNNI presented the priorities to the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC) and met with CAPAC Chair Rep. Grace Meng.
05 · RepeatEvery yearWe plan to do this annually, so the priorities stay current and progress can be tracked over time.
More than advocacy

This is how a community comes together to understand its own problems in depth and look for collective solutions. Bringing our priorities to Congress is one part of that work, not the whole of it.

Where resources should go

For nonprofits and policy organizations like NNI, the findings show where the gaps are widest, so we can bridge them and direct limited resources to the areas that need support most.

04 · In their own words

Asked for one message to Congress, about 4 in 10 raised immigration and visas.

Several called for removing the new $15,000 visa bond, which respondents said keeps families from visiting for milestones like graduations. Language came up far more in people’s own words than in the checkbox answers: many asked for reliable information and services in Mongolian.

“Our voices still don’t reach the policymaking level. What we need first is civic education and community representation.”
“Small business owners face so many barriers. There’s little support, especially with financing, and loans come with very high rates.”
“We need one official place, in Mongolian, to get the information and help to live, work, and study in the U.S.”

About this survey: 113 responses collected online May 3–12, 2026, in Mongolian and English. Respondents could pick up to three answers to each priority question, so totals exceed 100%. *Of 95 people who gave a clear yes or no. Open-ended themes were grouped by NNI and are approximate; quotes are translated or lightly edited and shared anonymously. This is a community survey, not a random sample.

The materials

What we brought to Capitol Hill.

During AAPI Heritage Month 2026, a community delegation organized by NNI presented the Mongolian American Community Policy Priority: Immigration Stability, Language Access, and Economic Integration to Rep. Grace Meng, Chair of the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC). It asks Congress for support in three areas.

01 · Economic integration

Small business and economic opportunity

Mongolian immigrants build small businesses and bring professional skills, but many hit barriers to capital, technical assistance, licensing, and mentorship.

The ask
  • Expanded access to SBA programs and workforce development
  • More outreach on entrepreneurship and financial literacy resources
  • Support for internationally trained professionals entering the workforce
  • Targeted inclusion of Mongolian Americans in AAPI economic empowerment initiatives
02 · Language access

Language access and community services

With few organizations serving Mongolian speakers, families often rely on relatives for interpretation in immigration, health, legal, and school settings.

The ask
  • Expanded Mongolian-language interpretation and translation
  • Inclusion of Mongolian speakers in federal language-access initiatives
  • Culturally responsive outreach to smaller immigrant communities
  • Capacity-building support for grassroots immigrant-serving organizations
03 · Immigration

Family reunification and travel

Recent restrictions and visa bonds of up to $15,000 on Mongolian B1/B2 applicants make lawful family visits and short business travel out of reach for many families.

The ask
  • Reevaluation of restrictions on Mongolian B1/B2 visa applicants
  • Review of nonimmigrant visa bond requirements
  • Transparent, evidence-based review of nationality-based restrictions
  • Support to establish a DMV Mongolian American Community Center
The bottom line

Fair, proportionate, evidence-based policy that recognizes both the small size of the Mongolian American community and its contributions — and keeps smaller immigrant communities in the conversation.

The delegation also voiced support for collecting data on AAPI business borrowers by race, ethnicity, and gender, so discrimination in lending can be identified, because access to capital remains one of the community’s most pressing issues.
