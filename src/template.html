Stronger communities begin with access.

New Neighbor Initiative helps communities, especially Mongolian American, AAPI, and immigrant groups, and small businesses turn existing opportunities into practical progress — by making institutions easier to navigate, connecting people with the right resources and relationships, developing leaders, and creating pathways into civic and economic life.

Our Approach

Access is more than knowing that a resource exists.

A grant, lender, procurement program, technical-assistance provider, or government initiative has little value to a business that does not know whether it fits, what is required, or how to act on it.

NNI works between opportunity and implementation. We help communities and entrepreneurs identify the right resources, understand requirements, strengthen readiness, connect with qualified institutions and partners, and translate those connections into practical next steps.

About NNI

Community-rooted. Institutionally connected.

New Neighbor Initiative closes the distance between communities, especially Mongolian American, AAPI, and immigrant groups, and the institutions, relationships, and opportunities that influence their lives — through civic engagement, leadership development, economic opportunity, and cultural programming.

Our Mission

Expand access, participation, leadership, and opportunity for communities, especially Mongolian American, AAPI, and immigrant groups.

Our Vision

Communities equipped with the knowledge, relationships, and opportunities to participate fully in civic and economic life.

Community Priorities Survey · May 2026

What 113 Mongolians across the U.S. told us.

In May 2026, Mongolian Americans came together through a series of town halls and a rapid pulse survey to name the challenges our community faces and what we want Congress to hear. The result is the Mongolian American Community Policy Priorities.

How these priorities came together

From community town halls to the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus.

01 · ConveneTown hallsMongolian community members met in a series of town halls to talk through the challenges they face.
02 · ListenRapid pulse surveyA short survey in Mongolian and English reached 113 people across 17 states and D.C.
03 · AnalyzeVolunteer-led analysisCommunity volunteers processed the data together and drafted the Mongolian American Community Policy Priorities.
04 · EngageOn Capitol HillNNI presented the priorities to the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC) and met with CAPAC Chair Rep. Grace Meng.
05 · RepeatEvery yearWe plan to do this annually, so the priorities stay current and progress can be tracked over time.
More than advocacy

This is how a community comes together to understand its own problems in depth and look for collective solutions. Bringing our priorities to Congress is one part of that work, not the whole of it.

Where resources should go

For nonprofits and policy organizations like NNI, the findings show where the gaps are widest, so we can bridge them and direct limited resources to the areas that need support most.

04 · In their own words

Asked for one message to Congress, about 4 in 10 raised immigration and visas.

Several called for removing the new $15,000 visa bond, which respondents said keeps families from visiting for milestones like graduations. Language came up far more in people’s own words than in the checkbox answers: many asked for reliable information and services in Mongolian.

“Our voices still don’t reach the policymaking level. What we need first is civic education and community representation.”
“Small business owners face so many barriers. There’s little support, especially with financing, and loans come with very high rates.”
“We need one official place, in Mongolian, to get the information and help to live, work, and study in the U.S.”

About this survey: 113 responses collected online May 3–12, 2026, in Mongolian and English. Respondents could pick up to three answers to each priority question, so totals exceed 100%. *Of 95 people who gave a clear yes or no. Open-ended themes were grouped by NNI and are approximate; quotes are translated or lightly edited and shared anonymously. This is a community survey, not a random sample.

The materials

What we brought to Capitol Hill.

During AAPI Heritage Month 2026, a community delegation organized by NNI presented the Mongolian American Community Policy Priority: Immigration Stability, Language Access, and Economic Integration to Rep. Grace Meng, Chair of the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC). It asks Congress for support in three areas.

01 · Economic integration

Small business and economic opportunity

Mongolian immigrants build small businesses and bring professional skills, but many hit barriers to capital, technical assistance, licensing, and mentorship.

The ask
  • Expanded access to SBA programs and workforce development
  • More outreach on entrepreneurship and financial literacy resources
  • Support for internationally trained professionals entering the workforce
  • Targeted inclusion of Mongolian Americans in AAPI economic empowerment initiatives
02 · Language access

Language access and community services

With few organizations serving Mongolian speakers, families often rely on relatives for interpretation in immigration, health, legal, and school settings.

The ask
  • Expanded Mongolian-language interpretation and translation
  • Inclusion of Mongolian speakers in federal language-access initiatives
  • Culturally responsive outreach to smaller immigrant communities
  • Capacity-building support for grassroots immigrant-serving organizations
03 · Immigration

Family reunification and travel

Recent restrictions and visa bonds of up to $15,000 on Mongolian B1/B2 applicants make lawful family visits and short business travel out of reach for many families.

The ask
  • Reevaluation of restrictions on Mongolian B1/B2 visa applicants
  • Review of nonimmigrant visa bond requirements
  • Transparent, evidence-based review of nationality-based restrictions
  • Support to establish a DMV Mongolian American Community Center
The bottom line

Fair, proportionate, evidence-based policy that recognizes both the small size of the Mongolian American community and its contributions — and keeps smaller immigrant communities in the conversation.

The delegation also voiced support for collecting data on AAPI business borrowers by race, ethnicity, and gender, so discrimination in lending can be identified, because access to capital remains one of the community’s most pressing issues.

Our role

NNI does not replace lenders, accountants, attorneys, procurement specialists, government programs, or other technical experts. We help businesses understand when those resources are relevant, prepare to use them, and connect with the right provider.

B2C → B2B

From a customer base to a market strategy.

A microbusiness may begin by serving individual customers. Growth can require learning how to sell the same expertise differently — to a corporation, nonprofit, university, local government, federal agency, or prime contractor. NNI helps owners evaluate those possibilities, understand what institutional buyers require, and determine which pathway is realistic for their stage of business.

Both sides of the gap

NNI works on both sides of the access gap.

With businesses

We identify practical barriers, clarify objectives, explain requirements, connect owners with appropriate resources, and support next steps.

With institutions

We share what we learn from direct engagement with businesses: where awareness breaks down, where requirements are unclear, where referrals fail, and where valuable programs are hard for microbusinesses to use.

Story 1 · Beauty & salon

Lashiskin Beauty Spa: from the treatment room to the retail shelf

lashiskin.com ↗
2small-business loans secured
2new full-time jobs and a second location
~2.5×revenue vs. previous level (owner-reported)

Lashiskin Beauty Spa built its business one appointment at a time. To reach more customers, the spa worked with New Neighbor Initiative (NNI) to explore a new source of revenue: developing and selling its own beauty products.

NNI helped Lashiskin connect that idea to a financing plan. The business secured two small-business loans to cover product development, manufacturing, packaging, and launch. Lashiskin then brought its own product line to market alongside its salon services.

As the business grew, Lashiskin opened a second location and added two full-time jobs. The business reported that revenue reached about two and a half times its previous level.

Story 2 · Staffing & B2B

MNG Staffing: from serving individual customers to winning a subcontract

mngstaffing.com ↗
B2C → B2Bnew market
D.C.metro prime and county procurement contacts
Firstsubcontract secured

MNG Staffing started out serving individual customers. When the team decided to pursue work with other businesses, it faced a practical question: How could a small staffing company get in front of the contractors who hire firms like theirs?

New Neighbor Initiative (NNI) worked with MNG to build a strategy around its staffing services and the opportunities it wanted to pursue. Together, they identified which procurement platforms to register on, which licenses to obtain, and how to build relationships with prime contractors and county procurement offices across the Washington, D.C. metro area. NNI also helped MNG engage with business organizations, including the Asian American Chamber of Commerce, to expand its network.

MNG put the strategy into practice and secured a subcontract. The win gave the company experience it could point to in future conversations with prime contractors and a clearer path to pursuing its next opportunity.

Privacy

Privacy policy

New Neighbor Initiative respects your privacy. This page explains what we collect through this website and how we use it.

What we collect

Only what you choose to send us: your name, email, and the details you enter in our contact, business help, or newsletter forms. Donations are processed by our donation platform, which handles your payment details; NNI does not see or store your card information.

How we use it

To answer your inquiry, follow up on a request for help, send the newsletter you signed up for, and send donation receipts. We do not sell or rent your information.

Your choices

You can unsubscribe from our newsletter at any time, or ask us to update or delete your information by emailing connect@nneighbor.org.

Survey data

Community survey results published on this site are shared only as group totals. Names, contact details, and other identifying information are never published.
